# 16 — HERO ART DIRECTION (cinematic treatment)

**Project:** Velmoré Hotel & Spa · **Phase:** Gate 2 — art direction (before design)
**What this is:** the **Creative Director's treatment** of the chosen Primary Hero — *"The Glass Hall at Dusk, Held"* (`docs/15`). It art-directs the **first screen as a cinematic moment**, dimension by dimension, and introduces the **Hero Cinematic Narrative**. **This is not UI, layout, components, wireframes, mockups or code.** It exists so the Hero feels *inevitable* before a single element is designed.
**Measured against:** [`BKB`](../knowledge-base/BUSINESS-KNOWLEDGE-BASE.md) v0.2 · **Creative Concept "The Confident Host"** (`docs/13`) · **North Star** — *"Every occasion — and everyone in it — held in capable, caring hands"* (`docs/14`) · **Visual Language** (`docs/12`) · content rules (`onboarding §13`).
**Subject:** the **D'Charmant Glass Hall at dusk** — glass architecture, chandeliers, 360° gardens, Italian-Tuscan styling — the client's strongest single image. *(onboarding §11.)*

---

## The intent, in one line
> **A guest arrives at dusk to find the room already lit and waiting for them.**
Warmth held within grandeur; the calm certainty of being expected. Not "look at this venue" — *"you are received."*

---

## The frame, dimension by dimension
Each dimension is explored, then **committed** (▸), then justified.

**Time of day** — ▸ **Dusk / the blue hour**, the moment the sky has cooled to deep estate-blue and the chandeliers have just been lit. *Why:* the whole emotion lives in one contrast — **warm light within, cool calm without** — the host lighting the room for you. It is also the client's explicit steer. Never flat midday.

**Lighting** — ▸ **Practical, warm, layered.** The chandeliers are the key (≈2700K, a soft glow with a whisper of bloom); the blue-hour sky is the cool fill/rim on the glass; low garden and path lights are warm accents that lead the eye in. *Why:* light that *welcomes* rather than displays; warmth = hospitality, the cool frame = the estate's quiet grandeur. No hard flash, no HDR, no over-saturation.

**Camera angle** — ▸ **Eye-level to a touch below, three-quarter, looking in through the glass from the garden approach** — the point of view of a guest walking up at dusk. *Why:* this is the *arrival* — you are received, not shown a brochure. Slightly-low lends the architecture grandeur without arrogance; human eye-height keeps it intimate and *stood-in*, never a detached drone shot.

**Lens feeling** — ▸ **Expansive framing, intimate depth** — a cinematic wide (~28–35mm feel), enough to hold the glass hall and the garden, but with natural perspective and a shallow-ish plane so it feels like *standing there*, not a real-estate wide-angle. *Why:* the Hero must be **both** at once — expansive to close the gap (grander than expected), intimate to feel warm (hospitality). No fisheye drama, no distortion.

**Atmosphere** — ▸ **Poised and expectant** — the settled hush of an estate exhaling into evening; the faintest air (a soft bloom on the lights, perhaps a breath of mist off the gardens). *Why:* "the room is prepared and waiting for your occasion." Calm, not empty; warm, not busy.

**Scale** — ▸ **Quiet grandeur** — read through the height of the glass, the chandeliers receding in a row, the garden extending beyond the frame. *Why:* the gap-close needs scale to register, but elegance forbids bombast. Grand because it *is*, never because it shouts. (Never show non-sellable assets — this is a sellable hero venue.)

**Architecture** — ▸ **The glass hall is the hero; let it lead.** A slightly **off-axis** composition that reveals both the glass façade and the depth *into* the hall. *Why:* dead-centre symmetry reads ceremonial/formal (a fine alternative for a wedding-specific hero); a slight asymmetry reads editorial and inviting, and creates a way *in*. Italian-Tuscan detailing present but never fussy.

**Human presence** — ▸ **Implied, not literal — the evidence of a host, not a model.** The chandeliers lit, doors open, a table set (no alcohol/bar — a served, halal-appropriate setting), warm light spilling onto the garden: *someone has prepared this, and you are expected.* **Sanctioned option:** a *single, small, out-of-focus* real figure at the far threshold (a staff member mid-preparation, or a couple in silhouette) purely for scale and the "received" feeling — never the subject, never stock, never a crowd. *Why:* "real over stock" is absolute; the host is *felt*, the architecture stays the hero. *(docs/12; onboarding §13.)*

**Composition** — ▸ **Leading lines draw the eye in** — the garden path / the row of chandeliers / the glass mullions carry from a foreground anchor to the warm heart of the hall (the off-centre focal glow). The **lower region is kept calm and darker** (the dusk garden), so the frame naturally reserves a place for the promise and the invitation to breathe. *Why:* composition should sequence the eye and anticipate the copy's home without designing it (bottom-anchored, per the Hero System).

**Depth** — ▸ **Three planes.** Foreground garden (soft) → the glass threshold (sharp) → the chandelier-lit depth of the hall (glowing). *Why:* layered planes read richer than any shadow and imply *there is more beyond the frame* — the estate's breadth, felt.

**Focus** — ▸ **Sharpest on the lit interior seen through the glass** (chandeliers + the set space); the foreground garden slightly soft. *Why:* the eye is led to the promise itself — *the room, held and ready* — while the soft foreground invites you to step in.

**Visual hierarchy (of the image itself)** — ▸ the eye travels **glow → scale → threshold → calm**: first the warm chandelier light (emotion), then the glass architecture (capability/scale), then the garden threshold (arrival/hospitality), then the quiet lower space (where the action will live). *Why:* the photograph's own hierarchy delivers the concept's sequence — emotion, then capability, then welcome, then invitation — before a word is added.

**Breathing room** — ▸ **Earned negative space** — the dusk sky above and the calm garden below breathe, *balanced* by the weight of the glowing hall. *Why:* restraint reads as confidence only when something of real weight anchors it (OS Law 3). This calm is what lets one promise and one CTA feel unhurried.

**Movement** — ▸ **Almost none — and it must survive being removed.** The frame is a composed still that renders at first paint (the LCP image never animates in). At most, a *barely-there* ambient life: the warm light breathing, a slow 6–9s drift, or an imperceptible parallax of the garden foreground against the hall on scroll (≤12%, desktop only, never on text). If ever a video: muted, ≤8s, the poster identical to the still, the light shifting so slightly you'd only feel it. *Why:* slow-luxury; if you notice the motion, it's too much. *(docs/12 §13; reduced-motion honoured.)*

**Emotional pacing** — ▸ the first screen should **unfold over ~2–3 seconds**: glow (warmth) → recognition (a real, grand estate, prepared) → scale (grand *and* warm) → invitation (I could walk in). *Why:* this pacing *is* the Hero Cinematic Narrative below — the art direction sets it up so the narrative feels inevitable, not imposed.

## The Hero Cinematic Narrative
A construct for this project: **every Hero must answer four questions in sequence** — a felt story, not a layout. For this Hero:

**Frame 1 — What do I notice first?**
The **warm glow of the glass hall at dusk** — chandeliers lit behind glass against a deep-blue estate sky. A *feeling before a fact*: warmth, held within grandeur. *(Emotion / awe — the gap closed in the first instant.)*

**Frame 2 — What do I immediately understand?**
*This is a real, grand estate — prepared and waiting for an occasion — near me, and it hosts more than one kind of day.* The architecture, the set hall and the garden say "estate & venue"; the promise-led line and "Pretoria & Centurion" name the category and the place. *(Category + breadth + location clarity.)*

**Frame 3 — Why should I trust this?**
Because it **shows, not tells.** The photograph is real and un-fakeable; beneath the promise, a quiet line of specific proof — *1,300-seat hall · 50 beds · certified halal · one invoice* — and the **certified-halal welcome** given a place of honour. *(Capability shown, belonging — the confident host's competence.)*

**Frame 4 — What should I do next?**
**Come and see it.** One warm, unmissable invitation — *Book a viewing* — with quiet reassurance (free · no obligation · same-day reply). The host's *"come and see; we'll take it from here."* *(Ease — the North Star's promise, actioned.)*

The four frames map exactly onto the image's own visual hierarchy (glow → scale → threshold → calm) — which is why, once shot and composed this way, the Hero should feel **inevitable**.

## The mobile crop (4:5) — lean into intimacy
A separate, tighter dusk crop: hold the **chandelier glow + a slice of glass + a breath of garden**, closer and warmer — intimacy over expanse. Keep the calm lower region for the promise, one proof line and *Book a viewing* within the fold. Art-direct the crop; never CSS-scale the wide frame down. *(docs/12 §20.)*

---

## Anti-direction (what would break it)
No stock people; no crowds; no posed models. · No **alcohol/bar/cocktail** cues of any kind (a served, halal-appropriate setting only). · No non-sellable assets (old wing, pool, field, glass garden boardroom). · No flat midday light; no HDR; no over-saturation; no heavy overlay rescuing a weak frame. · No drone-detachment or ultra-wide distortion. · No empty-cold minimalism (grandeur must be *warm*). · Nothing that shouts — the tell of insecurity, not class.

---

## The shot note (for photography / the gap-fill shoot)
If the existing D'Charmant dusk frame matches this treatment, it is the Hero — select it. If a fresh capture is needed: shoot at **blue hour**, chandeliers lit, from the **garden approach at eye-level**, three-quarter, ~28–35mm, three planes of depth, a halal-appropriate set within, warm-within-cool, and **leave the lower third calm** for copy. Capture a **portrait 4:5** on the same evening for mobile. *(Feeds `docs/12 §5` image manifest / `docs/03` gap-fill.)*

## Why the Hero now feels inevitable
Every prior layer converges on this one frame: the **North Star** (the room *held* for your occasion), **The Confident Host** (you are *received*, capably and warmly), the **BKB** (the client's strongest asset, the certified-halal welcome, the one-invoice scale), the **Marketing strategy** (emotion that closes the gap *and* proof that converts the procurement buyer, → *Book a viewing*), and the **Visual Language** (dusk imagery, warm-within-cool, one champagne invitation, slow-luxury calm). Art-directed this way, the Hero is no longer a choice among options — it is the picture the whole project has been describing.
